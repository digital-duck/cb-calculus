Concept Book: Velocity

Calculus Ch03 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Velocity is the first derivative of position with respect to time, defined rigorously as the limit

$$v(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{s(t + \Delta t) - s(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{ds}{dt},$$

where $s : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ denotes the position function. This definition does more than assign a number to "how fast"; it encodes direction and instantaneous rate of change simultaneously, making velocity a signed, time-varying quantity rather than a scalar speed. The entire preceding chapter has been an ascent toward this object: we built limits to make the fraction well-posed, continuity to guarantee the function does not jump, and differentiability to ensure the limit exists. Velocity is the natural endpoint of that construction because it is the simplest physically meaningful derivative — the bridge between the static geometry of a position curve and the dynamic world of motion.

Worked example. Let $s(t) = 3t^2 - 4t + 1$ metres, $t$ in seconds. Then

$$v(t) = \frac{d}{dt}(3t^2 - 4t + 1) = 6t - 4.$$

At $t = 1\,\text{s}$, the object moves at $v(1) = 2\,\text{m\,s}^{-1}$ (rightward); at $t = \tfrac{2}{3}\,\text{s}$ it is momentarily at rest ($v = 0$), marking a turning point visible as a local minimum of $s$.

Theorem (Fundamental link to acceleration). If $v$ is itself differentiable, its derivative $a(t) = dv/dt = d^2s/dt^2$ is the acceleration. By the chain rule of differentiation applied twice, every smooth position function carries within it a full kinematic hierarchy: $s \xrightarrow{d/dt} v \xrightarrow{d/dt} a$. Newton's second law $F = ma$ then transforms this hierarchy into a law of force, connecting pure calculus to mechanics.

import sympy as sp
t = sp.Symbol('t')
s = 3*t**2 - 4*t + 1
v = sp.diff(s, t)
a = sp.diff(v, t)
print("v(t) =", v)          # 6t - 4
print("a(t) =", a)          # 6
print("v at t=1 =", v.subs(t, 1))

Velocity, then, is not a terminus but a launching point. Each derivative you take opens a new layer of physical meaning: acceleration reveals the forces shaping a trajectory, and from acceleration spring the differential equations that govern planetary orbits, vibrating strings, and electrical circuits. To see how a single additional differentiation transforms kinematics into dynamics, turn to the chapter on acceleration and follow one parabolic trajectory from first principles to Newton's second law.
