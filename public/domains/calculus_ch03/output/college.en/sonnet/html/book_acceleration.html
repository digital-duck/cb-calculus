Concept Book: Acceleration

Calculus Ch03 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Acceleration is the second derivative of position with respect to time, and it is precisely this second-order character that makes it the natural endpoint of the kinematic ladder. Velocity tells us how fast a quantity changes; acceleration tells us how fast the rate of change itself changes. This distinction is not merely linguistic. Newton's Second Law, $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$, asserts that force—the fundamental cause of motion in classical mechanics—is proportional to acceleration, not to velocity or position. Every differential equation governing a physical system that responds to forces is therefore written in the language of second derivatives. To understand acceleration is to hold the key that unlocks an enormous class of real-world models.

Worked Example. A particle moves along the $x$-axis with position $x(t) = 3t^3 - 12t^2 + 9t$ metres. Its velocity is

$$v(t) = x'(t) = 9t^2 - 24t + 9,$$

and its acceleration is

$$a(t) = x''(t) = 18t - 24.$$

Setting $a(t) = 0$ gives $t = \tfrac{4}{3}$ s, the instant at which the particle transitions from decelerating to accelerating. At this instant the velocity is $v\!\left(\tfrac{4}{3}\right) = 9\cdot\tfrac{16}{9} - 24\cdot\tfrac{4}{3} + 9 = 16 - 32 + 9 = -7\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, confirming that the particle is still moving in the negative direction even as it begins to speed up. Acceleration, not velocity, locates this qualitative turning point.

Key Theorem (Second Derivative Test). Let $f$ be twice differentiable on an open interval containing $c$. If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$, then $f$ has a local minimum at $c$; if $f''(c) < 0$, a local maximum. This theorem shows that acceleration-type information governs the curvature of any smooth function, making it indispensable in optimisation, economics, and machine learning loss landscapes alike.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, diff, solve, Rational

t = symbols('t')
x = 3*t**3 - 12*t**2 + 9*t
v = diff(x, t)
a = diff(v, t)
inflection = solve(a, t)
print(f"v(t) = {v}")
print(f"a(t) = {a}")
print(f"Zero-acceleration instant: t = {inflection[0]}")
print(f"Velocity there: {v.subs(t, inflection[0])}")
