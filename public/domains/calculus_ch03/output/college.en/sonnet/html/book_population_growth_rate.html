What Population Growth Rate Achieves

After building the machinery of sequences, limits, derivatives, and differential equations, the concept of population growth rate stands as the natural synthesis: it is the first application in which every preceding tool earns its place simultaneously. Informally, the population growth rate measures how rapidly a population changes relative to its current size. Formally, if $N(t)$ denotes population size at time $t$, the per-capita growth rate is

$$r(t) = \frac{1}{N(t)}\frac{dN}{dt}.$$

When $r$ is constant, this ordinary differential equation admits the closed-form solution $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$, the exponential growth model. The derivative $dN/dt$ quantifies instantaneous change; the division by $N$ rescales that change to a dimensionless rate comparable across populations of vastly different sizes—a normalization that recurs throughout applied mathematics.

Worked Example

Suppose a bacterial colony contains $N_0 = 500$ cells at $t = 0$ and doubles every $20$ minutes. The doubling time $T_d = 20$ gives $r = \ln 2 / T_d \approx 0.0347\ \text{min}^{-1}$. At $t = 60$ minutes,

$$N(60) = 500\, e^{0.0347 \times 60} \approx 500 \times 8 = 4000\ \text{cells}.$$

Verifying: three doublings in sixty minutes yield $500 \times 2^3 = 4000$. The exponential formula and the doubling-time formula are two expressions of the same underlying rate.

Key Theorem

Logistic Correction. When resources are finite, the Verhulst model replaces constant $r$ with a density-dependent rate:

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = rN\!\left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right),$$

where $K$ is the carrying capacity. The equilibria $N = 0$ (unstable) and $N = K$ (stable) follow from setting $dN/dt = 0$; stability is confirmed by linearization around each fixed point.

Lab Cell

from sympy import symbols, Function, dsolve, Eq, Rational, exp, plot

t, r, K, N0 = symbols('t r K N_0', positive=True)
N = Function('N')

logistic_ode = Eq(N(t).diff(t), r * N(t) * (1 - N(t)/K))
sol = dsolve(logistic_ode, N(t), ics={N(0): N0})
print(sol)

Running this cell produces the sigmoid solution $N(t) = K N_0 / (N_0 + (K - N_0)e^{-rt})$, whose graph transitions smoothly from near-exponential growth to saturation at $K$.

Invitation

Population growth rate is the gateway to an interconnected landscape of models: epidemic spread (where $N$ becomes the infected compartment in an SIR system), evolutionary game theory (where $r$ depends on genotype frequency), and ecological stability analysis (where coupled Lotka–Volterra equations generalize the single-species logistic). Each domain reshapes the same core equation to answer a different question. To see how infectious-disease modeling transforms population growth rate into a tool for predicting epidemic thresholds—and how the basic reproduction number $\mathcal{R}_0$ emerges directly from $r$—turn to Chapter 9.
