Concept Book: Population Growth Rate

Payoff

What the Concept Achieves

Every quantitative study of a changing population — whether of bacteria in a flask, loans in a portfolio, or atoms in a radioactive sample — reduces, at its core, to a single question: at what fractional rate is the population changing per unit time? The population growth rate provides the answer. Defined formally, if $N(t)$ denotes the size of a population at time $t$, the (instantaneous) per-capita growth rate is

$$r(t) = \frac{1}{N(t)}\frac{dN}{dt}.$$

When $r$ is constant, this differential equation integrates immediately to the exponential law $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$, making $r$ both an observable (measurable from data) and a parameter (governing future trajectories). The concept is therefore the natural endpoint of a chain that begins with sequences, advances through limits and derivatives, and arrives here: a single scalar that encodes the long-run fate of any self-reproducing system.

Worked Example

Suppose a bacterial culture contains $N_0 = 500$ cells at $t = 0$ and $N(1) = 1{,}500$ cells after one hour. Assuming exponential growth,

$$1{,}500 = 500\,e^{r \cdot 1} \implies r = \ln 3 \approx 1.099 \text{ h}^{-1}.$$

The doubling time follows immediately: $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / r \approx 0.631$ hours, or roughly 38 minutes.

Key Theorem

Malthusian Growth Theorem. Let $N : [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ satisfy $dN/dt = rN$ with $r \in \mathbb{R}$ constant and $N(0) = N_0 > 0$. Then $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$ is the unique solution, and the population grows without bound if $r > 0$, decays to zero if $r < 0$, and remains constant if $r = 0$.

Proof sketch. Separate variables: $dN/N = r\,dt$. Integrate both sides, apply the initial condition, and exponentiate. Uniqueness follows from the Picard–Lindelöf theorem applied to the Lipschitz-continuous right-hand side $f(t,N)=rN$.

Lab Cell

from sympy import *
t, r, N0 = symbols('t r N0', positive=True)
N = N0 * exp(r * t)

# Per-capita growth rate (should recover r)
per_capita = simplify(diff(N, t) / N)
print("Per-capita rate:", per_capita)

# Doubling time
doubling = solve(Eq(N, 2*N0), t)[0]
print("Doubling time:", doubling)

Invitation

The exponential model is a first approximation; real populations encounter resource limits, predators, and disease. The logistic refinement replaces the constant $r$ with $r\!\left(1 - N/K\right)$, where $K$ is the carrying capacity, and immediately connects population growth rate to epidemiology (the basic reproduction number $\mathcal{R}_0$), ecology (Lotka–Volterra predator–prey cycles), and financial mathematics (continuously compounded interest). To see how a single parameter $r$ threads through all of these domains — and how its sign alone determines whether a system thrives or collapses — turn to the next chapter, where we develop the logistic equation in full.
