Mathematical Model

A mathematical model is a description of a real-world system using mathematical language — equations, functions, variables, and relationships — so that we can analyze it, make predictions, and test decisions without needing to run expensive or dangerous experiments on the real thing.

Every model involves a deliberate simplification: you keep the features that matter most for your question and discard the rest. This is not a flaw; it is the whole point. A model of a falling ball ignores air resistance when the ball is dense and slow, because that detail barely affects the answer. The art is knowing what to drop.

The general modeling cycle looks like this:

  • Identify the real-world question and the quantities that drive it.
  • Translate those quantities into variables and write equations that connect them.
  • Solve or simulate the mathematical system.
  • Interpret the output back in real-world terms.
  • Validate against data; refine if the model is off.

    • Worked Example. A coffee shop sells cups of coffee at price $p$ dollars. From past sales data, demand follows $q(p) = 200 - 20p$, where $q$ is cups sold per day. Revenue is $R = p \cdot q(p) = 200p - 20p^2$. To find the price that maximizes revenue, set the derivative to zero:

    $$\frac{dR}{dp} = 200 - 40p = 0 \implies p = 5$$

    The model predicts the shop maximizes revenue at \$5 per cup, earning $R = 200(5) - 20(25) = \$500$ per day. Notice that the model ignores competition, seasonal variation, and cost — those simplifications are acceptable if the goal is a quick pricing estimate.

    Applying the cycle to a new problem. Suppose a population of bacteria doubles every 3 hours starting from $N_0 = 500$ cells. The natural model is exponential growth: $N(t) = N_0 \cdot 2^{t/3}$, where $t$ is in hours. How long until the population exceeds 10,000?

    $$500 \cdot 2^{t/3} = 10{,}000 \implies 2^{t/3} = 20 \implies \frac{t}{3} = \log_2 20 \approx 4.32 \implies t \approx 13 \text{ hours}$$

    The key skill is not the algebra — it is recognizing that "doubles every 3 hours" is the signature of exponential growth, choosing the right model family, then letting the math do the work.
