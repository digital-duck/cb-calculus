Definite Integral

A definite integral measures the net signed area between a function and the horizontal axis over a closed interval. Given a continuous function $f(x)$ on $[a, b]$, the definite integral is written

$$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx$$

and equals the limit of Riemann sums: slice $[a,b]$ into $n$ subintervals of width $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}$, sample $f$ at a point $x_i^$ in each slice, form the sum $\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^)\,\Delta x$, and let $n \to \infty$. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives the practical shortcut: if $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$ (meaning $F'(x) = f(x)$), then

$$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = F(b) - F(a).$$

The notation $\big[F(x)\big]_a^b$ is shorthand for the same evaluation.

Worked example. Find the area under $f(x) = 3x^2$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 4$.

An antiderivative is $F(x) = x^3$, so

$$\int_1^4 3x^2\, dx = \big[x^3\big]_1^4 = 4^3 - 1^3 = 64 - 1 = 63.$$

Because $f(x) \geq 0$ on this interval, the result is a plain area: 63 square units.

Sign matters. When $f$ dips below the $x$-axis, the integral subtracts that portion. For example, $\int_0^{2\pi} \sin x\, dx = 0$ because the positive arch from $0$ to $\pi$ and the negative arch from $\pi$ to $2\pi$ cancel exactly. If you want total area (not net), split at the zeros and add absolute values.

Problem-solving application. A car's velocity (in m/s) is $v(t) = 6t - t^2$ for $0 \leq t \leq 6$. How far does it travel?

First check: $v(t) \geq 0$ on $[0, 6]$ (the factor $(6-t)$ stays non-negative), so displacement equals total distance.

$$\int_0^6 (6t - t^2)\, dt = \left[3t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^6 = \left(108 - 72\right) - 0 = 36 \text{ meters.}$$

The key steps are always the same: find an antiderivative, evaluate at the upper and lower limits, subtract. Watch for sign changes if the function crosses zero — that is the one trap that sends most students to the wrong answer.
