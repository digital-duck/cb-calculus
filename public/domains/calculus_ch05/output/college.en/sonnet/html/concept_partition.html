Partition

Definition. A partition of a set $S$ is a collection of non-empty subsets $\{A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_k\}$ satisfying two conditions:

  • Disjoint: $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ for all $i \neq j$.
  • Exhaustive: $A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots \cup A_k = S$.

    • Each $A_i$ is called a block of the partition. The two conditions together mean every element of $S$ belongs to exactly one block — no element is missed and none is double-counted.

    Worked example: integers modulo 3. Let $S = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$. Group elements by their remainder when divided by 3:

    $$A_0 = \{0, 3, 6\},\quad A_1 = \{1, 4, 7\},\quad A_2 = \{2, 5, 8\}.$$

    Every element appears in exactly one block and $A_0 \cup A_1 \cup A_2 = S$, so $\{A_0, A_1, A_2\}$ is a valid partition. This particular construction — grouping by remainder — is called a residue class partition and underlies modular arithmetic.

    Problem-solving application: the Law of Total Probability. Partitions are the engine behind one of the most powerful probability tools. If $\{A_1, \ldots, A_k\}$ partitions a sample space $\Omega$, then for any event $B$:

    $$P(B) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} P(B \mid A_i)\,P(A_i).$$

    The partition guarantee makes this exact: every outcome in $B$ falls in one and only one block, so the sum captures all of $B$ with no overlap.

    Concrete scenario. A factory has three production lines contributing 50 %, 30 %, and 20 % of daily output, with defect rates of 2 %, 4 %, and 6 % respectively. What share of all items is defective?

    The three lines partition total output, so:

    $$P(\text{defective}) = (0.02)(0.50) + (0.04)(0.30) + (0.06)(0.20) = 0.010 + 0.012 + 0.012 = 0.034.$$

    Overall, 3.4 % of items are defective. The calculation is valid precisely because the lines are disjoint and exhaustive — every item comes from exactly one line.

    Checking your work. When building a partition, always verify both conditions explicitly. Overlapping blocks cause double-counting; missing elements leave gaps. Either mistake breaks the total-probability formula and corrupts the result. The habit of checking disjointness and exhaustiveness separately takes ten seconds and catches most partition errors before they propagate.
