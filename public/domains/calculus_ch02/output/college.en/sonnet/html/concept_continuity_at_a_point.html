Continuity At A Point

A function $f$ is continuous at a point $x = a$ when three conditions all hold simultaneously: the function is defined at $a$, the limit of $f(x)$ as $x \to a$ exists, and that limit equals the function's value. Formally:

$$f \text{ is continuous at } a \iff \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$$

Intuitively, continuity at a point means you can draw through that point without lifting your pen — no holes, no jumps, no vertical blowup exactly at $a$.

Worked Example

Consider $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}$. Is $f$ continuous at $x = 2$?

Check each condition:

  • Defined? Substituting $x = 2$ gives $\frac{0}{0}$, so $f(2)$ is undefined. The first condition fails immediately.

    • $f$ is not continuous at $x = 2$ — it has a removable discontinuity there. Notice, however, that $\lim_{x \to 2} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 2}(x + 2) = 4$ exists. The discontinuity is removable because defining $f(2) = 4$ would restore all three conditions.

    Now consider $g(x) = x^2 + 3$ at $x = 2$.

  • Defined: $g(2) = 7$. ✓
  • Limit exists: $\lim_{x \to 2}(x^2 + 3) = 7$. ✓
  • Limit equals value: $7 = 7$. ✓

    • $g$ is continuous at $x = 2$.

    Problem-Solving Application

    When a problem asks you to find constants that make a piecewise function continuous at a junction point, apply all three conditions as a system. Suppose

    $$h(x) = \begin{cases} ax + 1 & x < 3 \\ x^2 - b & x \geq 3 \end{cases}$$

    and you need continuity at $x = 3$. The left-hand limit is $3a + 1$, the right-hand limit (and the value, since $x = 3$ falls in the second piece) is $9 - b$. Setting them equal:

    $$3a + 1 = 9 - b \implies 3a + b = 8$$

    This single equation gives a family of solutions; a second constraint (such as $h(3) = 7$) pins down both constants. The strategy generalizes: translate "continuous at $a$" into an equation by equating left-hand limit, right-hand limit, and function value, then solve algebraically.

    This pattern appears throughout calculus — in the intermediate value theorem, differentiability arguments, and integral bounds — so internalizing the three-condition checklist pays dividends far beyond this definition alone.
