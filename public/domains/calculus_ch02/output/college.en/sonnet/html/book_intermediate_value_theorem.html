Concept Book: Intermediate Value Theorem

Payoff

The journey through continuity, limits, and the topology of the real line converges here. The Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) is the first theorem in analysis that turns an abstract structural property — continuity — into a concrete existential guarantee. It does not merely describe behavior; it asserts that certain values must exist, even when we cannot compute them explicitly.

Theorem (Intermediate Value Theorem). Let $f : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous. If $k$ is any real number strictly between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f(c) = k$.

The proof rests on the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$: define $S = \{x \in [a, b] : f(x) < k\}$, take $c = \sup S$, and use continuity to show $f(c)$ can be neither strictly less than nor strictly greater than $k$. The details are a rewarding exercise in $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ reasoning.

Worked Example. Let $f(x) = x^3 - x - 2$ on $[1, 2]$. Compute $f(1) = -2 < 0$ and $f(2) = 4 > 0$. Since $f$ is a polynomial (hence continuous everywhere), the IVT guarantees some $c \in (1, 2)$ with $f(c) = 0$ — that is, the cubic has a real root in this interval. One can then apply the bisection method: evaluate $f(1.5) = -0.125 < 0$, so the root lies in $(1.5, 2)$; evaluate $f(1.75) \approx 1.11 > 0$, narrowing to $(1.5, 1.75)$. Each step halves the uncertainty, a consequence purely of the IVT applied repeatedly.

from sympy import *
x = symbols('x')
f = x**3 - x - 2
root = nsolve(f, x, 1.5)   # Newton refinement seeded by bisection bracket
print(f"Approximate root: {root:.6f}")

The IVT is the theoretical engine behind root-finding algorithms used throughout numerical analysis, fixed-point iteration, and the proof that every polynomial of odd degree over $\mathbb{R}$ has at least one real root. It underlies equilibrium arguments in economics (intermediate prices must be attained), topological fixed-point theorems such as Brouwer's, and guaranteed convergence proofs in optimization.

You have now seen how a single continuity assumption, combined with the completeness of the real line, yields existence results of profound reach. Choose any one application above — perhaps the bisection algorithm applied to a physical model from your own field — and carry it to its conclusion. The tools are in your hands.
