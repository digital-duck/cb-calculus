Concept Book: Curve Sketching

Calculus Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Curve sketching is the moment when calculus ceases to be a collection of isolated techniques and becomes a unified lens for understanding the behaviour of functions. Throughout this text you have computed limits to locate discontinuities, evaluated derivatives to measure rates of change, and applied the second derivative to classify curvature. Curve sketching demands all of these simultaneously: given a function $f$, you must determine its domain, intercepts, asymptotes, intervals of increase and decrease, local extrema, intervals of concavity, and inflection points, then synthesise every piece of information into a coherent geometric portrait. The skill is therefore not additive — it is integrative.

Why it is the natural endpoint. Each earlier concept in this book answered a local question: what is the slope here? or does this limit exist? Curve sketching asks the global question: what is the complete shape of this function? It forces you to reconcile information that can, and often does, conflict. A function may be increasing on an interval where it is concave down; a critical point may be neither a local maximum nor a local minimum. Resolving such tensions requires the full vocabulary you have built.

Worked synthesis. Consider $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$. The first derivative $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x-1)(x+1)$ vanishes at $x = \pm 1$, giving critical points. The second derivative $f''(x) = 6x$ is negative at $x = -1$, confirming a local maximum of $f(-1) = 2$, and positive at $x = 1$, confirming a local minimum of $f(1) = -2$. Since $f''(0) = 0$ and the sign of $f''$ changes there, the point $(0, 0)$ is an inflection point. End behaviour follows from the leading term: $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$. These facts together determine the sketch uniquely.

Key theorem (First Derivative Test). Let $c$ be a critical point of $f$. If $f'$ changes from positive to negative at $c$, then $f(c)$ is a local maximum; if from negative to positive, a local minimum; if $f'$ does not change sign, $c$ is neither.

import sympy as sp
x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x**3 - 3*x
critical = sp.solve(sp.diff(f, x), x)
inflections = sp.solve(sp.diff(f, x, 2), x)
print("Critical points:", critical)
print("Inflection points:", inflections)
sp.plot(f, (x, -3, 3), title="f(x) = x³ − 3x")
