Concept Book: Propagated Error

Calculus Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Every quantitative science rests on measurement, and every measurement carries uncertainty. The concept of propagated error is the answer to a question that arises the moment one computes anything from measured data: if each input is known only approximately, how uncertain is the output? Having built the machinery of partial derivatives, linear approximation, and variance, we are now in a position to give that question a precise, computable answer.

Definition. Let $f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ be a differentiable function of measured quantities $x_1, \dots, x_n$, each carrying an absolute uncertainty $\delta x_i$. The propagated (absolute) uncertainty in the derived quantity $z = f(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is

$$\delta z = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\right)^2 (\delta x_i)^2},$$

where the partial derivatives are evaluated at the measured (central) values. This expression follows from linearising $f$ about the central point and treating the individual uncertainties as independent, zero-mean random errors.

Worked example. A physicist measures a cylinder's radius $r = 3.00 \pm 0.05\ \text{cm}$ and height $h = 10.0 \pm 0.2\ \text{cm}$, then computes the volume $V = \pi r^2 h$.

$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial r} = 2\pi r h, \qquad \frac{\partial V}{\partial h} = \pi r^2.$$

Substituting the central values:

$$\delta V = \sqrt{(2\pi \cdot 3.00 \cdot 10.0)^2 (0.05)^2 + (\pi \cdot 3.00^2)^2 (0.2)^2} \approx 10.0\ \text{cm}^3.$$

The relative uncertainty $\delta V / V \approx 3.5\%$, dominated by the radius term because $V$ depends on $r$ quadratically.

Key theorem (Propagation in quadrature). If the input errors are statistically independent and small relative to the scale on which $f$ varies, the formula above gives the standard deviation of $z$ to first order in $\delta x_i$. When errors are correlated, off-diagonal covariance terms $\sigma_{ij}$ enter as $\sum_{i,j} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j}\sigma_{ij}$, recovering the full covariance-propagation formula of mathematical statistics.

Lab cell.

from sympy import *

r, h, dr, dh = symbols('r h delta_r delta_h', positive=True)
V = pi * r**2 * h
dV = sqrt((diff(V, r)*dr)**2 + (diff(V, h)*dh)**2)
dV_num = dV.subs({r: 3, h: 10, dr: 0.05, dh: 0.2})
print("δV =", float(dV_num.evalf()), "cm³")

Propagated error is the natural endpoint of this book because it transforms every earlier tool — differentiation, linear approximation, and statistical independence — into a single operational verdict on the reliability of a result. The applications it unlocks span the full breadth of quantitative work: in experimental physics, it certifies whether two measurements agree within combined uncertainty; in engineering tolerance analysis, it propagates manufacturing variation through a mechanical assembly; in geophysical surveying, it quantifies how GPS position errors compound into derived elevation maps; and in data science, it underpins confidence intervals for regression-derived predictions. In each case, the formula does not merely flag that error exists — it locates which input contributes most and thereby guides where to invest precision.
