Concept Book: Applied Optimization

Calculus Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Applied optimization is the discipline of locating values of decision variables that extremize an objective function, subject to constraints imposed by physical, economic, or logical reality. Where earlier chapters developed the machinery — gradients, Lagrange multipliers, convexity, and duality — this capstone assembles those tools into a coherent problem-solving framework applicable wherever a quantitative goal must be pursued under limited resources.

What applied optimization achieves. Every well-posed optimization problem reduces to the same logical skeleton: identify a feasible set $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, choose an objective $f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, and find $x^ = \arg\min_{x \in \mathcal{F}} f(x)$. The power of this abstraction is breadth: the same first-order necessary condition $\nabla f(x^) = 0$ (or its constrained analogue, the KKT system) governs portfolio allocation, neural-network training, and bridge-truss design alike. Mastering one framework therefore yields leverage across an entire landscape of quantitative disciplines.

Worked example. A manufacturer produces two goods with unit profits $p_1 = 5$ and $p_2 = 8$. Labor capacity imposes $2x_1 + 3x_2 \leq 120$ and material capacity imposes $x_1 + x_2 \leq 50$, with $x_1, x_2 \geq 0$. Maximizing $f = 5x_1 + 8x_2$ over this polytope, the simplex algorithm (or corner enumeration) yields the optimum at $x^ = (30, 20)$ with $f^ = 310$. Every step — modeling, feasibility, optimality — mirrors the general recipe.

Key theorem (Weierstrass Existence). If $f$ is continuous and $\mathcal{F}$ is nonempty and compact, then $f$ attains both its minimum and maximum on $\mathcal{F}$. This result underpins practical solver design: confirming compactness and continuity before computing guarantees that the search for $x^*$ is not chasing a limit that does not exist.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, Rational, linsolve, Matrix

x1, x2 = symbols('x1 x2', nonneg=True)
# Corner points of the feasible polytope
corners = [(0,0),(50,0),(30,20),(0,40)]
profit  = lambda a,b: 5*a + 8*b
best    = max(corners, key=lambda p: profit(*p))
print(f"Optimal corner: {best}, Profit: {profit(*best)}")
