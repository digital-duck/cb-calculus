Concept Book: Exponential Growth Model

Calculus Ch06 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Every concept introduced in this text — limits, derivatives, differential equations, and solutions to initial-value problems — converges here. The exponential growth model is not merely one more technique; it is the unique function that is its own rate of change, the fixed point of differentiation, and the canonical solution to the simplest nontrivial first-order linear ODE. Understanding it means understanding the mathematical signature that nature uses whenever a quantity changes in proportion to itself.

Definition. Let $y(t)$ denote a continuously differentiable quantity. The exponential growth model asserts that

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = k\,y(t), \qquad y(0) = y_0,$$

where $k \in \mathbb{R}$ is the growth rate and $y_0 > 0$ is the initial value. The unique solution is

$$y(t) = y_0\,e^{kt}.$$

When $k > 0$ the quantity grows without bound; when $k < 0$ it decays to zero; when $k = 0$ it is constant.

Worked example. A bacterial colony contains $500$ cells at time $t = 0$ and doubles every $3$ hours. Find the population at $t = 9$ hours.

From the doubling condition, $y_0 e^{3k} = 2y_0$, giving $k = \tfrac{\ln 2}{3}$. At $t = 9$:

$$y(9) = 500\,e^{9 \cdot \frac{\ln 2}{3}} = 500\,e^{3\ln 2} = 500 \cdot 2^3 = 4{,}000 \text{ cells.}$$

Key theorem (uniqueness). Among all continuously differentiable functions satisfying $y' = ky$ with $y(0) = y_0 > 0$, the function $y_0 e^{kt}$ is the only solution. This follows directly from the existence-uniqueness theorem for first-order linear ODEs with constant coefficients, guaranteeing that the exponential is not merely a solution but the solution.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, Function, dsolve, Eq, exp, ln, solve

t, k, y0 = symbols('t k y0', positive=True)
y = Function('y')
ode = Eq(y(t).diff(t), k * y(t))
sol = dsolve(ode, y(t), ics={y(0): y0})
print(sol)

# Doubling-time example
k_val = solve(Eq(y0 * exp(3*k), 2*y0), k)[0]
print("Growth rate:", k_val, "≈", float(k_val.subs(ln(2), 0.6931)))

The breadth of what this model unlocks is remarkable. With a single equation and a single parameter, you can describe compound interest accruing in a savings account, the decay of carbon-14 used to date archaeological artifacts, the early phase of an epidemic before saturation effects appear, the cooling of a hot object toward ambient temperature (via $k < 0$), and the amplification of a signal through a cascade of proportional stages. Each domain is governed by the same underlying structure; only the interpretation of $k$ and the units of $t$ change.
