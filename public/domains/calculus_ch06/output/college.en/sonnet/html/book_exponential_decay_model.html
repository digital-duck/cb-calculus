Concept Book: Exponential Decay Model

Calculus Ch06 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Definition. The exponential decay model describes any quantity $N(t)$ whose instantaneous rate of change is proportional to its current value with a negative constant of proportionality $-\lambda$, $\lambda > 0$:

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = -\lambda N, \qquad N(0) = N_0.$$

Separating variables and integrating yields the closed-form solution

$$N(t) = N_0 \, e^{-\lambda t},$$

where $\lambda$ is called the decay constant and $\tau = 1/\lambda$ is the characteristic time — the elapsed time after which $N$ falls to $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$ of its initial value. The half-life $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / \lambda$ is often the more physically intuitive parameter.

Worked Example. Suppose a radioactive sample begins with $N_0 = 1000$ atoms and has decay constant $\lambda = 0.05\ \text{s}^{-1}$. After $t = 20\ \text{s}$:

$$N(20) = 1000\, e^{-0.05 \times 20} = 1000\, e^{-1} \approx 368 \text{ atoms}.$$

The half-life is $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / 0.05 \approx 13.86\ \text{s}$, consistent with observing roughly half the sample intact at that moment.

Key Theorem (Uniqueness of Exponential Decay). Among all continuous functions satisfying $dN/dt = -\lambda N$ with $\lambda > 0$, the unique solution is $N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$. This follows immediately from the existence-uniqueness theorem for first-order linear ODEs: the right-hand side $f(t, N) = -\lambda N$ is Lipschitz continuous in $N$, guaranteeing a unique global solution.

Connection to Newton's Law of Cooling. Newton's Law of Cooling states that the temperature difference $\Delta T(t) = T(t) - T_{\text{env}}$ between an object and its surrounding environment satisfies

$$\frac{d(\Delta T)}{dt} = -k\,\Delta T, \qquad k > 0.$$

This is precisely the exponential decay equation with $\lambda = k$, yielding $\Delta T(t) = \Delta T_0\, e^{-kt}$. The model explains why a cup of coffee cools quickly at first and more slowly as it approaches room temperature — the driving force (the temperature gap itself) diminishes exponentially. Every parameter carries physical meaning: $k$ encodes the thermal conductivity and surface geometry of the object, and $\tau = 1/k$ is the time constant of the thermal system.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import *
t, lam, N0, k, DT0 = symbols('t lambda N_0 k DeltaT_0', positive=True)

N = N0 * exp(-lam * t)
DeltaT = DT0 * exp(-k * t)

print("General decay solution:", N)
print("Newton cooling solution:", DeltaT)
print("Half-life:", solve(Eq(N, N0/2), t))
