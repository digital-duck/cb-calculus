Exponential Growth Model

Definition. A quantity $N(t)$ exhibits exponential growth when its rate of change is proportional to its current value. Formally, $N$ satisfies the ordinary differential equation

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = r\,N(t), \qquad N(0) = N_0,$$

where $r > 0$ is the growth rate (units: time$^{-1}$) and $N_0 > 0$ is the initial value. Separating variables and integrating both sides yields the closed-form solution

$$N(t) = N_0\,e^{rt}.$$

The quantity $T_{2} = \ln 2 / r$ is called the doubling time: the elapsed time required for $N$ to double from any starting value.

Worked Example. A bacterial colony begins with $N_0 = 500$ cells and doubles every $3$ hours. Determine $r$ and predict the population after $12$ hours.

Step 1 — find $r$. The doubling time is $T_2 = 3$ h, so

$$r = \frac{\ln 2}{T_2} = \frac{\ln 2}{3} \approx 0.2310 \text{ h}^{-1}.$$

Step 2 — evaluate $N(12)$.

$$N(12) = 500\,e^{0.2310 \times 12} = 500\,e^{2.773} \approx 500 \times 16 = 8000 \text{ cells}.$$

This result is exact in integer arithmetic because $12/3 = 4$ doublings give $N_0 \cdot 2^4 = 8000$.

Key Theorem (Uniqueness of Exponential Growth). Let $N : [0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable and satisfy $dN/dt = rN$ with $N(0) = N_0 > 0$. Then $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$ is the unique solution.

Proof sketch. Define $f(t) = N(t)e^{-rt}$. Differentiating gives $f'(t) = N'(t)e^{-rt} - rN(t)e^{-rt} = (N' - rN)e^{-rt} = 0$. Hence $f$ is constant, and evaluating at $t = 0$ yields $f(t) = N_0$ for all $t \geq 0$. $\blacksquare$

import sympy as sp

t, r, N0 = sp.symbols('t r N_0', positive=True)
N = N0 * sp.exp(r * t)

# Verify the ODE
ode_check = sp.simplify(sp.diff(N, t) - r * N)
print("ODE residual:", ode_check)          # 0

# Doubling time
T2 = sp.solve(sp.Eq(N, 2 * N0), t)[0]
print("Doubling time:", T2)                # log(2)/r

# Numeric example
N_numeric = N.subs([(N0, 500), (r, sp.ln(2)/3)])
print("N(12) =", N_numeric.subs(t, 12))   # 8000
