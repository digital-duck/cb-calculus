Exponential Growth Model

Definition. Exponential growth describes a quantity that increases at a rate proportional to its current size. If $N(t)$ is the quantity at time $t$ and the growth rate is the constant $r$, this relationship is captured by the differential equation $\frac{dN}{dt} = rN$, whose solution is:

$$N(t) = N_0 \, e^{rt}$$

where $N_0 = N(0)$ is the initial value and $e \approx 2.718$ is Euler's number. The key feature is self-reinforcing growth: the larger $N$ becomes, the faster it grows. Doubling time — the time needed for $N$ to double — is a useful companion quantity: $t_{\text{double}} = \frac{\ln 2}{r}$.

Worked example. A bacterial culture starts with 500 cells and doubles every 3 hours. Find the population after 12 hours.

First, extract $r$ from the doubling time: $r = \frac{\ln 2}{3} \approx 0.231 \text{ hr}^{-1}$. Then apply the formula:

$$N(12) = 500 \cdot e^{0.231 \times 12} = 500 \cdot e^{2.773} \approx 500 \times 16 = 8{,}000 \text{ cells}$$

You can verify this by counting doublings: 12 hours ÷ 3 hours per doubling = 4 doublings, so $500 \times 2^4 = 8{,}000$. Both approaches agree.

Problem-solving application. Exponential models arise in finance, epidemiology, and ecology. The workflow for applying them has three steps.

  • Identify $r$ or $t_{\text{double}}$ from given data. If you are told "grows 8% per year," then $r = 0.08$ (continuous) or work with the discrete analog $N(t) = N_0 (1.08)^t$. These are slightly different; be clear which convention the problem uses.
  • Set up the equation for the unknown. To find when a population reaches a target $N^$, solve $N_0 e^{rt} = N^$, giving $t = \frac{\ln(N^*/N_0)}{r}$.
  • Sanity-check with doubling time. If your answer implies 30 doublings in a decade, pause — exponential models break down when resources are finite. That is the signal to switch to a logistic growth model instead.

    • A practical pitfall: confusing the continuous rate $r$ with the percentage growth quoted in everyday language. An investment "growing at 7% annually" uses a discrete compounding convention; the equivalent continuous rate is $r = \ln(1.07) \approx 0.0677$, not $0.07$.
