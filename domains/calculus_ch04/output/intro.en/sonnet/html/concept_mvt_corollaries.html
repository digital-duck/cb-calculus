Mvt Corollaries

Definition. The Mean Value Theorem (MVT) guarantees that if $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then there exists $c \in (a,b)$ such that $f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$. Several important results follow directly from this single fact, each narrowing or extending its conclusion under additional hypotheses.

Corollary 1 (Zero Derivative Implies Constant). If $f'(x) = 0$ for all $x \in (a,b)$, then $f$ is constant on $(a,b)$.

Proof sketch. Pick any $x_1, x_2 \in (a,b)$ with $x_1 < x_2$. By the MVT, there exists $c \in (x_1,x_2)$ such that $f(x_2) - f(x_1) = f'(c)(x_2 - x_1) = 0$. Hence $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$. $\square$

Corollary 2 (Equal Derivatives Imply Functions Differ by a Constant). If $f'(x) = g'(x)$ for all $x \in (a,b)$, then $f(x) - g(x) = C$ for some constant $C$.

Proof sketch. Apply Corollary 1 to $h(x) = f(x) - g(x)$, which satisfies $h'(x) = 0$. $\square$

This corollary is the theoretical backbone of the indefinite integral: antiderivatives are unique up to an additive constant.

Corollary 3 (Monotonicity Test). If $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x \in (a,b)$, then $f$ is strictly increasing on $(a,b)$; if $f'(x) < 0$, then $f$ is strictly decreasing.

Proof sketch. For $x_1 < x_2$ in $(a,b)$, the MVT gives $f(x_2) - f(x_1) = f'(c)(x_2 - x_1)$. Since $x_2 - x_1 > 0$ and $f'(c) > 0$, we conclude $f(x_2) > f(x_1)$. $\square$

Worked Example. Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ on $(-\infty, \infty)$. Then $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x-1)(x+1)$. Corollary 3 tells us $f$ is increasing on $(-\infty,-1)$ and $(1,\infty)$ (where $f' > 0$) and decreasing on $(-1,1)$ (where $f' < 0$).

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, diff, solve, oo, Interval, S

x = symbols('x')
f = x**3 - 3*x
fp = diff(f, x)
critical = solve(fp, x)          # [-1, 1]
print("f'(x) =", fp)
print("Critical points:", critical)
# Check sign on (1, oo)
print("f'(2) =", fp.subs(x, 2)) # 9 > 0 → increasing
