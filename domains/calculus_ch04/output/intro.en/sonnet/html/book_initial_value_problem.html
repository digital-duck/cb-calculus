Concept Book: Initial Value Problem

Calculus Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

An initial value problem (IVP) is a differential equation paired with a prescribed state at a specific instant. Formally, given a function $f : \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$, an IVP asks for a differentiable curve $\mathbf{y} : I \to \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfying

$$\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{dt} = f(t, \mathbf{y}), \qquad \mathbf{y}(t_0) = \mathbf{y}_0,$$

where $t_0$ is the initial time and $\mathbf{y}_0$ is the initial state vector. The IVP is the natural endpoint of this textbook because every concept developed here — limits, derivatives, integrals, linear systems, and series — converges on this single formulation. Knowing a law of change (the differential equation) and a starting condition (the initial data) is, in a precise sense, the minimum information needed to determine a system's entire future trajectory.

Worked example. Consider a body cooling in a room held at $T_{\text{env}} = 20\,^{\circ}\text{C}$, governed by Newton's law of cooling:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{env}}), \qquad T(0) = 90.$$

Separating variables and integrating yields

$$T(t) = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt}.$$

With $k = 0.05\,\text{min}^{-1}$, the body reaches $37\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ after approximately $t = \frac{1}{0.05}\ln\!\left(\frac{70}{17}\right) \approx 28.5$ minutes — a calculation with direct forensic significance.

Theorem (Picard–Lindelöf). If $f$ is continuous in $t$ and Lipschitz-continuous in $\mathbf{y}$ on a rectangle containing $(t_0, \mathbf{y}_0)$, then the IVP has a unique solution on some interval $[t_0 - \delta,\, t_0 + \delta]$. This existence-and-uniqueness guarantee is what permits confident numerical simulation: we are not approximating a phantom curve.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, Function, dsolve, Eq, exp
t, k = symbols('t k', positive=True)
T = Function('T')
ode = Eq(T(t).diff(t), -k*(T(t) - 20))
sol = dsolve(ode, T(t), ics={T(0): 90})
print(sol)

Running this cell recovers $T(t) = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt}$ symbolically, confirming the hand calculation in seconds.

The IVP framework reaches far beyond cooling bodies. It underlies epidemic models (SIR systems), orbital mechanics (Newton's gravitational equations), electrical circuits (RC and RLC equations), and population dynamics (logistic growth). Each domain supplies its own $f$ and $\mathbf{y}_0$; the mathematical structure, and the guarantee of Picard–Lindelöf, remain identical across all of them.
