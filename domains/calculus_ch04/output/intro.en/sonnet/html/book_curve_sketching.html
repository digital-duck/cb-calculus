Concept Book: Curve Sketching

Calculus Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Curve sketching is the synthesis of nearly every analytic tool introduced in a first course in differential calculus. Given a function $f : I \subseteq \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, the goal is to produce a faithful qualitative portrait of its graph — capturing domain, intercepts, asymptotes, monotonicity, concavity, and extrema — without plotting thousands of individual points. In doing so, curve sketching transforms a list of local computational facts into a single coherent geometric object. This is not merely a drawing exercise; it is the discipline of reading a function's global behavior from its derivatives.

Worked Example. Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 + 1}$. The domain is $\mathbb{R}$. Since $f(-x) = f(x)$, the graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis. Setting $f(x) = 0$ gives $x = \pm 1$; evaluating $f(0) = -1$ locates the $y$-intercept. As $x \to \pm\infty$, $f(x) \to 1$, so $y = 1$ is a horizontal asymptote. Computing $f'(x) = \dfrac{4x}{(x^2+1)^2}$ shows $f$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, 0)$ and increasing on $(0, \infty)$, with a global minimum at $(0, -1)$. The second derivative $f''(x) = \dfrac{4(1 - 3x^2)}{(x^2+1)^3}$ vanishes at $x = \pm 1/\sqrt{3}$, yielding inflection points where the curve transitions from concave up to concave down. These six pieces of information uniquely determine the shape; no additional points are needed.

Key Theorem (First and Second Derivative Tests). If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$, then $f$ has a strict local minimum at $c$; if $f''(c) < 0$, a strict local maximum. If $f''(c) = 0$, the test is inconclusive and sign analysis of $f'$ in a punctured neighborhood of $c$ is required.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import *
x = symbols('x')
f = (x**2 - 1) / (x**2 + 1)
print(limit(f, x, oo), solve(f.diff(x)), solve(f.diff(x, 2)))
plot(f, (x, -4, 4))
