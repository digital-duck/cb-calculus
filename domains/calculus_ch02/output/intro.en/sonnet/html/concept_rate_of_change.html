Rate Of Change

Definition. Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a real-valued function. The average rate of change of $f$ over an interval $[a, b]$ is the ratio

$$\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$

The instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at a point $x = a$ is the limit of this ratio as the interval shrinks to zero:

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h},$$

provided this limit exists. When it does, $f$ is said to be differentiable at $a$, and $f'(a)$ is called the derivative of $f$ at $a$. Geometrically, $f'(a)$ is the slope of the tangent line to the graph of $f$ at the point $(a,\, f(a))$.

Worked Example. Consider $f(x) = x^2$. Compute the instantaneous rate of change at $x = 3$.

$$f'(3) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(3+h)^2 - 9}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{9 + 6h + h^2 - 9}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (6 + h) = 6.$$

Thus the tangent line at $(3, 9)$ has slope $6$ and equation $y - 9 = 6(x - 3)$, i.e., $y = 6x - 9$.

Key Theorem (Power Rule). For any $n \in \mathbb{R}$, if $f(x) = x^n$, then

$$f'(x) = n\,x^{n-1}.$$

Proof sketch. Apply the binomial theorem to $(x+h)^n$, expand, divide by $h$, and take $h \to 0$. All terms containing a positive power of $h$ vanish, leaving $n x^{n-1}$. $\square$

The power rule, together with linearity of the derivative, reduces differentiation of any polynomial to an entirely algebraic procedure.

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x**2
f_prime = sp.diff(f, x)          # Power rule applied symbolically
slope_at_3 = f_prime.subs(x, 3)

print("f'(x) =", f_prime)        # 2*x
print("f'(3) =", slope_at_3)     # 6

# Visualise the tangent line
tangent = slope_at_3 * (x - 3) + f.subs(x, 3)
print("Tangent line: y =", sp.expand(tangent))  # 6*x - 9

Running this cell confirms the analytic result and illustrates how symbolic computation can verify hand calculations before proceeding to more complex functions.
