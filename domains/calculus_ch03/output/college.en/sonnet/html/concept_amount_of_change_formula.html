Amount Of Change Formula

Definition. When a quantity changes continuously over time, its total change over an interval equals the definite integral of its rate of change. If $Q(t)$ is any differentiable function and $Q'(t)$ is its derivative, then:

$$Q(b) - Q(a) = \int_a^b Q'(t)\, dt$$

This is a direct consequence of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and is sometimes called the Net Change Theorem. The left side is simply the arithmetic difference — the ending value minus the starting value. The right side accumulates all the instantaneous rates of change across the interval. They are equal. That equality is the power of the formula: you can recover total change from rate-of-change data even when you never know $Q(t)$ explicitly.

Worked example. A car's velocity (in miles per hour) at time $t$ hours is given by $v(t) = 3t^2 - 6t + 4$. How far does the car travel between $t = 1$ and $t = 3$?

Distance is the amount of change in position $s(t)$, and $s'(t) = v(t)$, so:

$$\Delta s = \int_1^3 (3t^2 - 6t + 4)\, dt$$

Evaluate term by term:

$$= \left[ t^3 - 3t^2 + 4t \right]_1^3 = (27 - 27 + 12) - (1 - 3 + 4) = 12 - 2 = 10 \text{ miles}$$

The car travels 10 miles. Notice we never needed an explicit formula for $s(t)$ — just its derivative and two endpoints.

Problem-solving application. The formula applies whenever you know a rate and need a total. A water tank drains at $r(t) = 5 - 0.4t$ gallons per minute. How much water leaves the tank in the first 10 minutes?

$$\Delta W = \int_0^{10} (5 - 0.4t)\, dt = \left[ 5t - 0.2t^2 \right]_0^{10} = (50 - 20) - 0 = 30 \text{ gallons}$$

Key problem-solving steps:

  • Identify the quantity $Q$ whose change you need.
  • Confirm that the given function is $Q'(t)$, the rate of change of that quantity.
  • Set up and evaluate the definite integral over the specified interval.

    • Watch for sign: if $Q'(t) < 0$ on part of the interval, the integral correctly reduces the total, reflecting a decrease in $Q$. Always interpret the sign of the result in context before reporting it as a final answer.
