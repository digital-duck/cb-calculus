What Marginal Functions Achieve

Every concept introduced in the preceding chapters—limits, derivatives, the algebra of rates—converges here. A marginal function associated with a quantity $Q(x)$ is its derivative $Q'(x)$, interpreted not as an abstract slope but as the instantaneous rate of change of $Q$ per unit increase in $x$. Formally:

Definition. Let $Q : D \subseteq \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable on an open interval. The marginal function of $Q$ is

$$M_Q(x) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{Q(x + \Delta x) - Q(x)}{\Delta x} = Q'(x).$$

Where $Q$ represents cumulative output, cost, revenue, or any accumulated quantity, $M_Q(x)$ answers the practical question: what does one additional unit cost—or produce—right now?

Worked Example

Suppose total cost is $C(q) = 0.5q^3 - 4q^2 + 20q + 100$ (dollars, $q$ units). Then

$$MC(q) = C'(q) = 1.5q^2 - 8q + 20.$$

At $q = 4$: $MC(4) = 1.5(16) - 8(4) + 20 = 24 - 32 + 20 = 12$ dollars per unit. This means the firm spends approximately \$12 to produce its 4th unit, even though the average cost at that point is $C(4)/4 = 76/4 = 19$ dollars. The divergence between marginal and average cost is precisely what drives optimal production decisions.

Key Theorem

Necessary condition for interior optimality. If $\pi(q) = R(q) - C(q)$ is profit and $q^*$ is an interior maximum, then

$$M_R(q^*) = M_C(q^*),$$

that is, marginal revenue equals marginal cost. The proof follows immediately from Fermat's theorem: $\pi'(q^) = 0 \Rightarrow R'(q^) - C'(q^*) = 0$.

Lab Cell (SymPy)

from sympy import symbols, diff, solve, Rational

q = symbols('q', positive=True)
C = Rational(1,2)*q**3 - 4*q**2 + 20*q + 100
R = 30*q                          # competitive price = 30

MC = diff(C, q)
MR = diff(R, q)

q_star = solve(MR - MC, q)
print("Optimal q:", q_star)
print("MC at optimum:", MC.subs(q, q_star[0]))

An Invitation

Marginal analysis is the shared grammar of economics, thermodynamics, pharmacokinetics, and machine learning—wherever a system accumulates something, its marginal function governs when to stop, when to invest further, and where equilibrium lies. Choose any one of these domains and ask: what is the quantity being accumulated, and what does its derivative reveal? That question, now fully within your reach, is the beginning of genuine applied mathematics.
