Concept Book: Richter Scale Application

Payoff

Throughout this chapter, the machinery of logarithms has been assembled piece by piece: the definition of $\log_b x$ as the inverse of exponentiation, the change-of-base identity, and the algebraic laws that compress products into sums. The Richter scale application is the natural capstone because it deploys every one of these tools in a single, consequential formula and forces the student to interpret the result in physical terms.

What the Richter Scale Achieves. The moment magnitude (a modern refinement of Richter's original scale) assigns to an earthquake a dimensionless number $M$ defined by

$$M = \frac{2}{3}\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{E}{E_0}\right),$$

where $E$ is the seismic energy released in joules and $E_0 = 10^{4.8}$ J is a fixed reference energy. The logarithm is indispensable here: seismic energies span more than twenty orders of magnitude, from the barely perceptible tremor at $E \approx 10^{6}$ J to the catastrophic rupture at $E \approx 10^{18}$ J. A linear scale would be numerically useless for communicating that difference; the logarithmic compression renders it as the interval $[0.8, 9.5]$ on $M$.

Worked Example. The 1906 San Francisco earthquake released approximately $E_1 = 10^{17}$ J. The 1994 Northridge earthquake released approximately $E_2 = 10^{15}$ J. Compute the difference in magnitude.

$$M_1 - M_2 = \frac{2}{3}\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{E_1}{E_2}\right) = \frac{2}{3}\log_{10}(10^2) = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 2 = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33.$$

A difference of 1.33 magnitude units corresponds to an energy ratio of $10^{1.33\cdot 3/2} = 10^2 = 100$: the 1906 event released one hundred times more energy than Northridge. The logarithm translates an unwieldy hundred-fold ratio into a compact decimal offset.

Key Theorem (Logarithmic Difference Rule). For any two energies $E_1, E_2 > 0$,

$$M_1 - M_2 = \frac{2}{3}\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{E_1}{E_2}\right).$$

Consequently, each unit increase in $M$ corresponds to an energy increase by a factor of $10^{3/2} \approx 31.6$.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import log, Rational, N

E0 = 10**Rational(24, 5)          # reference energy 10^4.8
def magnitude(E):
    return Rational(2, 3) * log(E / E0, 10)

E_SF    = 10**17                   # 1906 San Francisco
E_NR    = 10**15                   # 1994 Northridge
print(N(magnitude(E_SF), 4))       # ≈ 7.93
print(N(magnitude(E_NR), 4))       # ≈ 6.60
print(N(magnitude(E_SF) - magnitude(E_NR), 4))  # ≈ 1.33

The Richter scale stands at the intersection of seismology, signal processing, and public risk communication — every domain in which vast dynamic ranges must be made legible. Having mastered the algebra that underlies it, you are equipped to enter any of those fields and read the mathematics behind the headline numbers. Choose one: open a seismological database, retrieve a catalog of recent events, and ask what fraction of the total energy released in a given year was contributed by the single largest earthquake. The answer, computed with nothing more than the tools in this chapter, will surprise you.
