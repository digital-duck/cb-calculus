Compound Interest Application

Definition. Let $P$ denote the principal (initial deposit), $r$ the nominal annual interest rate, $n$ the number of compounding periods per year, and $t$ the time in years. The compound interest formula gives the accumulated value $A$ as

$$A = P\!\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}.$$

When compounding occurs continuously—that is, as $n \to \infty$—the expression converges to

$$A = Pe^{rt},$$

where $e \approx 2.71828$ is Euler's number. This limiting form follows directly from the standard limit $\lim_{n\to\infty}\!\left(1+\tfrac{x}{n}\right)^n = e^x$.

Worked Example. Suppose you deposit $\$5{,}000$ at a nominal annual rate of $6\%$. Compare the accumulated value after $10$ years under (a) monthly compounding and (b) continuous compounding.

(a) Monthly compounding ($n = 12$):

$$A = 5000\!\left(1 + \frac{0.06}{12}\right)^{12 \times 10} = 5000\,(1.005)^{120} \approx \$9{,}096.98.$$

(b) Continuous compounding:

$$A = 5000\,e^{0.06 \times 10} = 5000\,e^{0.6} \approx \$9{,}110.18.$$

The difference of roughly $\$13$ illustrates that continuous compounding is the theoretical upper bound; in practice, the gap between daily and continuous compounding is negligible.

Key Theorem (Doubling Time). Under continuous compounding at rate $r$, the time required for any principal to double satisfies

$$2P = Pe^{rt^*} \implies t^* = \frac{\ln 2}{r}.$$

This is the celebrated Rule of 70 approximation: $t^ \approx 70/r_{\%}$, where $r_{\%} = 100r$. For $r = 6\%$, the exact value is $t^ = \ln 2 / 0.06 \approx 11.55$ years, while the rule gives $70/6 \approx 11.67$ years—an error of less than $0.2\%$.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, exp, ln, solve, N

P, r, t = symbols('P r t', positive=True)

# Continuous compounding formula
A = P * exp(r * t)

# Doubling time: solve A = 2P for t
t_double = solve(A - 2*P, t)[0]
print("Doubling time:", t_double)          # ln(2)/r

# Numerical example: r=6%, t=10 yr, P=5000
print("A =", N(A.subs({P: 5000, r: 0.06, t: 10}), 6))

Running this cell confirms $t^* = \ln(2)/r$ symbolically and returns $A \approx 9110.18$ for the numerical example, matching the hand calculation above.
