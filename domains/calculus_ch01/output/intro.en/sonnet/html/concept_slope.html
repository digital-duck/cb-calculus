Slope

Definition. Let $f$ be a function defined on an open interval containing points $x_1$ and $x_2$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$. The slope of the secant line through $(x_1, f(x_1))$ and $(x_2, f(x_2))$ is the ratio

$$m = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}.$$

When $f$ is differentiable at a point $x_0$, the slope of the tangent line at $x_0$ is the limit of secant slopes as the two points coalesce:

$$m_{\text{tan}} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + \Delta x) - f(x_0)}{\Delta x} = f'(x_0).$$

Slope carries a precise geometric meaning: it measures the signed rate of vertical change per unit of horizontal displacement. A positive slope indicates that $f$ increases from left to right; a negative slope indicates decrease; zero slope corresponds to a horizontal tangent, a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for a local extremum.

Worked example. Let $f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2$. We compute the slope of the tangent line at $x_0 = 2$.

Applying the limit definition:

$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(2+h)^2 - 3(2+h) + 2 - (4 - 6 + 2)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{4 + 4h + h^2 - 6 - 3h + 2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0}(h + 1) = 1.$$

The tangent line at $(2, 0)$ therefore has equation $y = x - 2$.

Key theorem (Mean Value Theorem). If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that

$$f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$

In other words, the instantaneous slope equals the average slope at least once on every such interval. This theorem underpins the connection between local derivative information and global function behavior.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, diff, limit, simplify

x, h = symbols('x h')
f = x**2 - 3*x + 2

# Derivative via limit definition
slope_def = limit((f.subs(x, 2 + h) - f.subs(x, 2)) / h, h, 0)
print("Slope at x=2 (limit):", slope_def)

# Symbolic derivative
fp = diff(f, x)
print("f'(x) =", fp)
print("f'(2) =", fp.subs(x, 2))

Running this cell confirms $f'(2) = 1$ and displays the general derivative $f'(x) = 2x - 3$, which equals zero at $x = \tfrac{3}{2}$—the vertex of the parabola.
