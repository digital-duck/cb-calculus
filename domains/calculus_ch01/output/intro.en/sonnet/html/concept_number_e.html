Number E

Definition. The number $e$ is the unique real number satisfying

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n = e \approx 2.71828\ldots$$

Equivalently, $e$ is the base of the natural exponential function $f(x) = e^x$, characterized by the property that $f'(x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. A third equivalent definition uses the natural logarithm: $e$ is the unique positive real number such that $\int_1^e \frac{1}{t}\,dt = 1$. All three definitions are provably equivalent and each illuminates a different facet of this fundamental constant.

Worked Example. Compute $\left(1 + \frac{1}{1000}\right)^{1000}$ and compare with $e$.

$$\left(1 + 0.001\right)^{1000} = (1.001)^{1000} \approx 2.71692\ldots$$

Taking the natural logarithm: $1000 \cdot \ln(1.001) = 1000 \cdot (0.001 - \frac{0.001^2}{2} + \cdots) \approx 0.9995$, so the expression evaluates to $e^{0.9995} \approx 2.7169$. The error $|e - 2.7169| \approx 0.00138$ confirms that convergence to $e$ is real but slow — the error decreases as $O(1/n)$.

Key Theorem (Irrationality and Transcendence of $e$). The number $e$ is irrational and transcendental.

Proof sketch (irrationality). Suppose $e = p/q$ for integers $p, q > 0$. Consider the truncated exponential series $S_q = \sum_{k=0}^{q} \frac{1}{k!}$. Multiplying by $q!$ yields $q!\, e - q!\, S_q = q! \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!}$. The left side is an integer (by assumption), while the right side satisfies

$$0 < q! \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} < \frac{1}{q} \leq \frac{1}{1} = 1,$$

a contradiction. Hence $e \notin \mathbb{Q}$. Transcendence (Hermite, 1873) follows from a more involved polynomial argument and implies $e$ cannot satisfy any nonzero polynomial equation with rational coefficients.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import E, exp, limit, symbols, oo, Rational, log, N

n = symbols('n', positive=True)

# Limit definition
print(limit((1 + 1/n)**n, n, oo))          # E

# Self-derivative property
x = symbols('x')
f = exp(x)
print(f.diff(x) == f)                       # True

# Integral definition: int_1^e (1/t) dt = 1
t = symbols('t', positive=True)
from sympy import integrate
print(integrate(1/t, (t, 1, E)))            # 1

# Numerical approximation
print(N(E, 50))
