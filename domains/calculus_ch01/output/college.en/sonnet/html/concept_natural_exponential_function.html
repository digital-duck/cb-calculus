Natural Exponential Function

The natural exponential function is $f(x) = e^x$, where $e \approx 2.71828$ is Euler's number. What makes $e$ special is that $e^x$ is its own derivative — the rate at which $e^x$ grows at any point equals the value of the function at that point. This self-referential property makes it the natural model for any process where the growth rate is proportional to the current size: populations, radioactive decay, compound interest, and cooling.

The domain is all real numbers, the range is $(0, \infty)$, and the graph is always increasing with a horizontal asymptote at $y = 0$ as $x \to -\infty$.

Worked example. A bacterial culture starts with 500 cells and doubles every 20 minutes. Model the population $P(t)$ using the natural exponential function, where $t$ is measured in minutes.

Doubling every 20 minutes means the growth rate constant $k$ satisfies $e^{k \cdot 20} = 2$, so:

$$k = \frac{\ln 2}{20} \approx 0.0347 \text{ min}^{-1}$$

The model is:

$$P(t) = 500 \, e^{0.0347t}$$

At $t = 60$ minutes (three hours): $P(60) = 500 \, e^{0.0347 \times 60} = 500 \, e^{2.08} \approx 500 \times 8.00 = 4000$ cells. This matches the expected result of $500 \times 2^3 = 4000$, confirming the model.

Problem-solving application. Newton's Law of Cooling states that an object's temperature approaches the ambient temperature exponentially:

$$T(t) = T_{\text{env}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{env}}) \, e^{-kt}$$

Suppose a cup of coffee at $90°C$ sits in a $20°C$ room. After 10 minutes it measures $70°C$. Find the temperature at 30 minutes.

First, solve for $k$:

$$70 = 20 + 70 \, e^{-10k} \implies e^{-10k} = \frac{50}{70} \implies k = -\frac{\ln(5/7)}{10} \approx 0.0336$$

Then at $t = 30$:

$$T(30) = 20 + 70 \, e^{-0.0336 \times 30} = 20 + 70 \, e^{-1.008} \approx 20 + 25.5 \approx 45.5°C$$

The key problem-solving moves are always the same: identify the initial value, find $k$ from one data point using $\ln$, then substitute the target time. Recognizing the exponential structure of a scenario — growth or decay proportional to current value — is the signal to reach for $e^x$.
