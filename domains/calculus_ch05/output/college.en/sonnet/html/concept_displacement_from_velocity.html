Displacement From Velocity

Definition. Displacement measures the net change in position — where you end up relative to where you started, with direction. Velocity is the rate at which position changes with time. Connecting the two: if you know an object's velocity at every instant, you can recover its displacement by accumulating those instantaneous changes over a time interval.

For a constant velocity $v$, the relationship is simply

$$\Delta x = v \cdot \Delta t$$

But when velocity varies with time, you cannot multiply a single number by elapsed time. Instead, displacement is the definite integral of velocity over the interval $[t_1, t_2]$:

$$\Delta x = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v(t)\, dt$$

Geometrically, this is the signed area between the velocity curve and the time axis. Area above the axis contributes positive displacement; area below contributes negative displacement. Distance traveled — unlike displacement — sums the absolute value of both regions and is always non-negative.

Worked Example. A cyclist's velocity (in m/s) is given by $v(t) = 6t - t^2$ for $0 \leq t \leq 6$ s. Find the displacement over the full six seconds.

$$\Delta x = \int_0^6 (6t - t^2)\, dt = \left[3t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^6 = \left(3(36) - \frac{216}{3}\right) - 0 = 108 - 72 = 36 \text{ m}$$

The cyclist ends up 36 m ahead of the starting point. (The velocity is non-negative on $[0,6]$, so displacement and distance happen to be equal here.)

Problem-Solving Application. Suppose a drone has velocity $v(t) = 4 - 2t$ m/s for $0 \leq t \leq 4$ s. The drone moves forward while $v > 0$ (i.e., $t < 2$) and backward while $v < 0$ ($t > 2$).

Displacement:

$$\Delta x = \int_0^4 (4 - 2t)\, dt = \left[4t - t^2\right]_0^4 = 16 - 16 = 0 \text{ m}$$

The drone returns exactly to its start — net displacement is zero.

Distance traveled requires splitting the integral at $t = 2$:

$$d = \int_0^2 (4 - 2t)\, dt + \int_2^4 |4 - 2t|\, dt = 4 + 4 = 8 \text{ m}$$

The key habit: always check where $v(t) = 0$ to decide whether you need to split the integral. For displacement, integrate straight through; for distance, integrate each sign-consistent piece separately.
