Fundamental Theorem Calculus Part2

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2 answers a practical question that haunted early mathematicians: instead of approximating areas with infinitely many rectangles, can we find an exact answer by working backwards from differentiation? The answer is yes, and the result is one of the most useful computational tools in all of mathematics.

Theorem (FTC, Part 2). If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$ (meaning $F'(x) = f(x)$), then

$$\int_a^b f(x)\, dx = F(b) - F(a).$$

In other words, the definite integral — which geometrically measures the net signed area under $f$ from $a$ to $b$ — can be evaluated exactly by evaluating any antiderivative at the two endpoints and subtracting. The shorthand $\bigl[F(x)\bigr]_a^b$ denotes $F(b) - F(a)$.

Worked Example. Compute $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (2x + 1)\, dx$.

Find an antiderivative: since the derivative of $x^2 + x$ is $2x + 1$, take $F(x) = x^2 + x$.

$$\int_1^3 (2x+1)\, dx = \bigl[x^2 + x\bigr]_1^3 = (9 + 3) - (1 + 1) = 12 - 2 = 10.$$

You can verify this geometrically: the region under the line $y = 2x + 1$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 3$ is a trapezoid with parallel sides of height $3$ and $7$ and width $2$, giving area $\tfrac{1}{2}(3 + 7)(2) = 10$. The theorem and geometry agree exactly.

Problem-Solving Application. Suppose a car's velocity is $v(t) = 3t^2 - 4t + 1$ m/s. How far does it travel from $t = 1$ to $t = 3$ seconds?

Net displacement equals $\displaystyle\int_1^3 v(t)\, dt$. An antiderivative is $F(t) = t^3 - 2t^2 + t$, so

$$\int_1^3 (3t^2 - 4t + 1)\, dt = \bigl[t^3 - 2t^2 + t\bigr]_1^3 = (27 - 18 + 3) - (1 - 2 + 1) = 12 - 0 = 12 \text{ m}.$$

The key skill is recognizing that integration is anti-differentiation: once you find $F$ such that $F' = f$, the definite integral collapses to a two-point evaluation. This turns an otherwise infinite limiting process into straightforward algebra — which is precisely why FTC Part 2 is the engine behind almost every integral calculation you will encounter.
