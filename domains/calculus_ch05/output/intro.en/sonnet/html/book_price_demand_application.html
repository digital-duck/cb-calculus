Payoff

What the Concept Achieves

Throughout this text we have built, layer by layer, the mathematical machinery of change: limits, derivatives, and the algebra of functions. The concept of price–demand application is the point at which that machinery turns outward and meets economic reality. It asks a precise question—how does the quantity demanded of a good respond to a change in its price?—and answers it with the tools we have assembled.

Formally, let $Q = f(P)$ be a demand function expressing quantity demanded as a function of price $P$. The price elasticity of demand at price $P_0$ is defined as

$$\varepsilon(P_0) = \frac{dQ}{dP}\bigg|_{P=P_0} \cdot \frac{P_0}{Q_0},$$

where $Q_0 = f(P_0)$. This dimensionless ratio measures the proportional sensitivity of demand to price, stripping away the accident of units.

Worked Example

Suppose $Q = 200 - 4P$. At $P_0 = 30$, we have $Q_0 = 200 - 4(30) = 80$ and $\tfrac{dQ}{dP} = -4$. Therefore

$$\varepsilon(30) = (-4)\cdot\frac{30}{80} = -\frac{3}{2}.$$

Because $|\varepsilon| = 1.5 > 1$, demand is elastic at this price: a one-percent increase in price reduces quantity by one-and-a-half percent, so total revenue $R = PQ$ falls. The derivative of revenue,

$$\frac{dR}{dP} = Q + P\frac{dQ}{dP} = Q(1 + \varepsilon),$$

confirms this: $\varepsilon < -1$ implies $\tfrac{dR}{dP} < 0$.

Key Theorem

Revenue Extremum Criterion. Revenue $R(P) = P \cdot f(P)$ is maximized at the price $P^$ satisfying $\varepsilon(P^) = -1$, provided $f$ is differentiable and $R$ is concave near $P^*$.

This theorem is the natural endpoint of our journey: it unites the limit definition of the derivative, the chain rule, and sign analysis of $\tfrac{dR}{dP}$ into a single actionable decision rule.

Lab Cell

from sympy import symbols, diff, solve, Rational

P, Q = symbols('P Q', positive=True)
f = 200 - 4*P
elasticity = diff(f, P) * P / f
P_star = solve(elasticity + 1, P)
print("Unit-elastic price:", P_star)          # [25]
print("Max-revenue quantity:", f.subs(P, P_star[0]))  # 100
