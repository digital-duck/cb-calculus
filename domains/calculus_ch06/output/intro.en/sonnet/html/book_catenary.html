Concept Book: Catenary

Calculus Ch06 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

The catenary is the curve assumed by a perfectly flexible, inextensible chain of uniform linear density suspended freely between two fixed points under gravity alone. Far from being a curiosity of statics, it is the geometric fingerprint of a profound variational principle: among all curves of fixed length joining two points, the catenary minimises the potential energy of the hanging mass. This extremal character elevates it from a mere shape to a necessary shape — one that nature selects without human instruction.

Definition. Place the lowest point of the chain at the origin. If $a = T_0/(\rho g)$, where $T_0$ is the horizontal tension, $\rho$ the linear density, and $g$ the gravitational acceleration, then the catenary is described by

$$y = a\cosh\!\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) = \frac{a}{2}\!\left(e^{x/a} + e^{-x/a}\right).$$

The parameter $a$ is the catenary constant: it sets the scale of curvature and equals the height of the lowest point above the directrix $y = 0$.

Worked example. A uniform chain of length $L = 10\,\text{m}$ is hung between two poles separated by $d = 8\,\text{m}$ at equal heights. To find the sag, note that the arc-length formula gives $L = 2a\sinh(d/2a)$, which must be solved numerically for $a$. With $d = 8$ and $L = 10$, one obtains $a \approx 3.868\,\text{m}$, and the midpoint sag is $\Delta y = a\cosh(0) - a\cosh(d/2a)^{-1}$... more precisely, the dip below the endpoints is $a[\cosh(d/2a) - 1] \approx 2.20\,\text{m}$.

Key theorem (Euler–Lagrange characterisation). The catenary is the unique solution to the variational problem $\delta\!\int \rho g\, y\,\mathrm{d}s = 0$ subject to fixed arc-length and fixed endpoints, where $\mathrm{d}s = \sqrt{1 + (y')^2}\,\mathrm{d}x$. Its curvature satisfies $\kappa = 1/(a\cosh^2(x/a))$, so the normal component of gravity exactly balances the tension gradient at every point.

Lab cell.

from sympy import *
x, a = symbols('x a', positive=True)
y = a * cosh(x / a)
arc_length = integrate(sqrt(1 + diff(y, x)**2), (x, -4, 4))
print(simplify(arc_length))   # 2*a*sinh(4/a)
