Catenary

Definition. A catenary is the plane curve assumed by a flexible, inextensible chain of uniform linear mass density suspended freely between two fixed endpoints under uniform gravity. Positioning the lowest point of the chain at the origin, the catenary is described by

$$y(x) = a\cosh\!\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) = \frac{a}{2}\!\left(e^{x/a}+e^{-x/a}\right),$$

where $a = T_0/(\rho g)$ is the catenary constant, $T_0$ is the horizontal component of tension (constant along the chain), $\rho$ is the linear mass density, and $g$ is gravitational acceleration. The parameter $a$ has units of length and controls how "flat" or "steep" the curve is: larger $a$ yields a flatter arch.

Worked Example. A chain of linear density $\rho = 2\ \text{kg/m}$ hangs between two posts. The horizontal tension at the vertex is $T_0 = 196\ \text{N}$. Find the catenary constant and the height of the chain above its lowest point at $x = 5\ \text{m}$.

Solution. The catenary constant is

$$a = \frac{T_0}{\rho g} = \frac{196}{2 \times 9.8} = 10\ \text{m}.$$

The height above the vertex at $x = 5\ \text{m}$ is

$$y(5) - y(0) = 10\cosh\!\left(\tfrac{5}{10}\right) - 10 = 10\cosh(0.5) - 10 \approx 10(1.1276) - 10 = 1.276\ \text{m}.$$

Key Theorem (Euler–Lagrange Characterisation). Among all curves of fixed arc length connecting two points, the catenary is the unique curve whose center of mass lies lowest — equivalently, it minimises potential energy. This follows from the calculus of variations: minimising $\int \rho g\, y\,\mathrm{d}s$ subject to the arc-length constraint $\int \mathrm{d}s = L$ via the Euler–Lagrange equation yields exactly $y'' = \tfrac{1}{a}\sqrt{1+(y')^2}$, whose solution is $y = a\cosh(x/a)$.

A notable corollary is that the catenary is not a parabola, despite their visual similarity; Galileo conjectured the parabola but was corrected by Leibniz, Huygens, and the Bernoulli brothers in 1691.

import sympy as sp

x, a = sp.symbols('x a', positive=True)
y = a * sp.cosh(x / a)

arc_length_integrand = sp.sqrt(1 + sp.diff(y, x)**2)
arc_length_integrand_simplified = sp.simplify(arc_length_integrand)

print("Arc-length element ds/dx =", arc_length_integrand_simplified)
print("y(5) with a=10 =", y.subs([(x, 5), (a, 10)]).evalf())
print("Height above vertex =", (y.subs([(x, 5), (a, 10)]) - y.subs([(x, 0), (a, 10)])).evalf())
